\section{Invertibility of Certain Matrices}

\textbf{(a)}
Assume \(\mu>0\), and suppose
\[
(A^TA+\mu I)z=0
\]
Multiply on the left by \(z^T\):
\[
z^T(A^TA+\mu I)z=0
\]
This gives
\[
z^TA^TAz+\mu z^Tz=0
\]
or
\[
\|Az\|_2^2+\mu\|z\|_2^2=0
\]
Both terms are nonnegative, and \(\mu>0\). Therefore \(\|z\|_2^2=0\), so
\[
z=0
\]
Thus the nullspace of \(A^TA+\mu I\) is zero. Since \(A^TA+\mu I\) is square, it is invertible. The same argument with \(A^T\) in place of \(A\) shows that
\[
AA^T+\mu I
\]
is also invertible.

\textbf{(b)}
First,
\[
A^T(AA^T+\mu I)=A^TAA^T+\mu A^T
\]
and
\[
(A^TA+\mu I)A^T=A^TAA^T+\mu A^T
\]
so
\[
A^T(AA^T+\mu I)=(A^TA+\mu I)A^T
\]
Multiplying on the left by \((A^TA+\mu I)^{-1}\) and on the right by \((AA^T+\mu I)^{-1}\) gives
\[
(A^TA+\mu I)^{-1}A^T
=
A^T(AA^T+\mu I)^{-1}
\]

\textbf{(c)}
Assume \(A\) is fat and full rank. Then \(AA^T\) is square and invertible. From part (b),
\[
(A^TA+\mu I)^{-1}A^T
=
A^T(AA^T+\mu I)^{-1}
\]
Taking \(\mu\to 0^+\), and using continuity of matrix inversion at the invertible matrix \(AA^T\),
\[
(AA^T+\mu I)^{-1}\to (AA^T)^{-1}
\]
Therefore
\[
(A^TA+\mu I)^{-1}A^T
\to
A^T(AA^T)^{-1}
\]
